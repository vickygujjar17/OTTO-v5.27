563: feat — change trail start to 1.0R and add High Table trailing health audit

RATIONALE

  v5.62 split the milestone ladder from the ATR trail gate so the trail could be
  retuned without darkening the ladder's first floor, and then set
  InpTrailStartRR to 2.0. That left an intentional one-R gap: between +1.0R and
  +2.0R the stop was carried by the milestone floors alone, with no ATR
  component. v5.63 closes that gap. The trail now arms on the SAME +1.0R rung as
  the ladder's first floor, so from +1.0R onward the basket wears BOTH the
  milestone floors and the ATR component and the tighter of the two wins on each
  tick.

  That change has a consequence the audit layer could not previously see: the
  two contributors to the basket stop now overlap for the whole life of a trade.
  A trail that silently arms and then never writes a stop is indistinguishable
  from a healthy one on any single tick, and with the trail armed from +1.0R
  there is no longer any tick at which the ladder is provably the only writer.
  v5.63 therefore adds an independent witness for exactly that condition, plus
  an aggregate-risk leg that the existing per-leg audit is structurally blind
  to.

COUPLING ACCEPTED: the +1.0R rung now reports STEP_TRAILING

  With InpTrailStartRR == InpBreakEvenRR == 1.0 the two classification branches
  in SyncTradeState() coincide. STEP_TRAILING is tested first, so the +1.0R rung
  reports STEP_TRAILING and the STEP_BREAKEVEN branch is shadowed while the two
  inputs are equal. This is deliberate and is now documented at the branch:

    * at +1.0R the ATR trail genuinely IS armed, so STEP_TRAILING is the
      truthful label;
    * the +1.0R milestone floor is still applied to the basket by the ladder
      block in Update() regardless of what the classifier reports, so no risk
      behaviour changes with the label;
    * InpCutRiskRR (also 1.0) is unaffected -- it sits BELOW both branches in
      the chain, behind its own m_cutRiskRungLive gate;
    * STEP_BREAKEVEN becomes reachable again the moment InpTrailStartRR is
      raised above InpBreakEvenRR.

  The probe pins the ORDERING, not just the values, because the ordering is what
  makes the label correct: swapping the two branches (a plausible tidy-up)
  immediately fails test_v563_trail_health.py.

SOURCE (continued)

  CHighTableAuditor.mqh -- AuditTrailingHealth() (new body, 1 site in RunAudit)
    Runs after AuditRiskExposure() in RunAudit(). It is the independent witness
    that an ARMED trail is actually tightening the basket.

    THE ANTI-CIRCULARITY RULE, which is the whole point of the design. The
    tempting test is "the stop reads +0.4R, so the trail must be lagging". That
    test is circular: the only reason a stop reads below the arm threshold is
    that it has not moved -- the very hypothesis under test. Worse, a fully
    frozen stop reads NEGATIVE R and would sail through it. So the excursion is
    taken from the LIVE PRICE every cycle (BID for a long, ASK for a short) and
    kept as a running maximum, m_trailPeakRR, across cycles. The arm test then
    compares the PEAK, never the stop:

      * peak < InpTrailStartRR      -> gate still closed, no stop movement owed
      * peak >= InpTrailStartRR     -> the trail is armed; the LIVE stop (read
                                       from the book, pushed value as fallback)
                                       must have left the losing side of entry

    A stop still on the losing side after the peak cleared the arm threshold, or
    no stop at all, raises a latched HT_SEV_WARN alert naming the ticket, the
    observed peak, the entry, the 1R and the stop's own R.

    Unjudgeable states stand the check DOWN rather than guessing: no geometry
    pushed, no tracked trade, DIR_NONE, or a non-positive price all re-arm the
    latch and return. Scope is deliberately narrow -- the body does not assert
    the stop equals the trail value, does not know the ATR and does not measure
    lag. Anything stricter would duplicate the trade manager's arithmetic here
    and drift from it silently.

  CHighTableAuditor.mqh -- SetTrackedGeometry() + NoteExcursion() (new ingress)
    The terminal can answer everything about a leg EXCEPT its 1R unit:
    m_basketRRUnit is the order layer's own private divisor, derived at basket
    init and persisted across restarts. Re-deriving it inside the auditor would
    create a second, silently drift-prone source of truth for the denominator of
    every RR decision -- exactly what the decoupling contract at the top of the
    file forbids -- so otto.mq5 pushes it alongside the entry, direction and
    working stop.

    A carrier-less push (no ticket, or a non-positive 1R) is recorded as NO
    geometry rather than as geometry of zero: a zero divisor would turn the
    stale-trail test into a permanent false CRITICAL. The ingress also resets
    m_trailPeakRR when the tracked ticket CHANGES, because a high-water mark
    carried over from the previous trade would be measured against a different
    entry and 1R. Resetting can never manufacture an alert (a zero peak is below
    every arm threshold) and costs at most one audit cycle of observation.

  CHighTableAuditor.mqh -- aggregate basket-risk leg in AuditRiskExposure()
    Strictly ADDITIVE. The per-leg peak (peakRiskPct) is blind to how many legs
    are stacked behind the shared stop: four correctly-sized legs can each sit
    comfortably inside the 1.5% per-leg allowance and still sum past what the
    pyramid was designed to carry. The same loop and the same loss-side guard
    now also accumulate sumRiskPct, tested against the stacked design budget

      RiskPercent + InpRiskT2Pct + InpRiskT3Pct + InpRiskT4Pct   (x 1.50 tolerance)

    so a correctly-sized pyramid at full size sits exactly at the allowance and
    never alerts. It has its OWN latch (m_alertSent_AggRiskBreach) and its own
    two-cycle confirmation (m_aggRiskSeen), for the same reason the trail check
    does: a tranche landing just before its stop is pushed reads high for one
    cycle, and that is the shape of a fill rather than an incident.

    Every v5.46 guarantee that test_v546_risk_isolation.py pins is preserved
    byte-for-byte: MathAbs remains gone, the signed lossDist and its
    `<= 0.0 continue` guard are untouched, maxAllowedRiskPct = RiskPercent *
    1.50 is unchanged, the peak is still the WORST leg keyed to the local
    ticket, moneyAtRisk = distPts * tickValue * vol is unchanged, and the
    per-leg latch still dispatches WARN and re-arms on a clean cycle.
